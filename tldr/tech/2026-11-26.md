### Big Tech & Startups

[### Neuralink receives FDA approval for 'pivotal' brain implant trial in ALS patients (2 minute read)](https://www.engadget.com/2329008/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-for-brain-implant-trial-in-als-patients/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

Neuralink has received permission from the FDA to launch a trial for its LINK brain-computer interface. The study will be open to a limited number of patients with ALS in the US. It will evaluate patients' ability to control digital devices through thought and assess the system's long-term safety and reliability. If the technology proves to be safe, the trial may allow Neuralink to apply for more advanced trials and eventually release LINK onto the market.

[### SpaceX Gets Approval to Launch One Million Satellites for Orbital Data Centers (4 minute read)](https://www.wsj.com/business/fcc-authorizes-spacex-to-build-1-million-data-centers-in-space-0eb20ee7?st=5CveM7&amp;reflink=desktopwebshare_permalink&amp;utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

The FCC has granted SpaceX approval to launch a million data centers into space. Anthropic and Google have already committed to paying billions of dollars in fees to rent capacity from the network. Proponents of orbital data centers say offloading advanced computing to orbit will allow developers of AI tools to avoid the headaches they are facing on Earth. There are still numerous hurdles to overcome for SpaceX to deploy data centers in space. The FCC authorization gives SpaceX until 2033 to launch and operate the first satellite and until 2040 to launch the full constellation.

### Science & Futuristic Technology

[### The short age of dragon riders (10 minute read)](https://atilloy.com/2026/11/16/the-short-age-of-dragon-riders/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

AI models available to the public have passed a threshold in August this year. They are now stronger than most theoretical physicists at most technical tasks. Models are now able to carry out complex analytical calculations, work out lengthy proofs, and turn well-posed problems into code with minimal input. There will still be joy in discoveries even if it is not only humans that push them.

[### SpaceX is not a monopoly, tech incumbents obviously lack urgency, and other Harsh Truths (49 minute read)](https://blog.caseyhandmer.com/2026/11/25/spacex-is-not-a-monopoly-tech-incumbents-obviously-lack-urgency-and-other-harsh-truths/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

The US space industry is generally unhealthy. The legacy industry lacks vigor. SpaceX's announced intention to gracefully sunset Falcon three years from now has induced a deep, spiraling panic among industry insiders who understand that there is no alternative. The company is uniquely positioned to put compute in space, and it has been investing heavily in this direction for more than a decade. Other tech giants have largely run out of ideas to invest for future growth. Due to this lack of vision, some have resorted to dividends and stock buybacks.

[### Late Mid-Game Software Engineering (7 minute read)](https://benjamincongdon.me/blog/2026/11/25/Late-Mid-Game-Software-Engineering/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

There is a growing frontier of work that can be fully black-box automated. There is still some work that seems resistant to being automated, but once you have a reasonably verifiable target, then the work to get to that target largely falls to agent teams/swarms/factories. Agents' ability to do problem selection, intent, and taste is improving. This means a continuous rediscovery of what pieces require human input, and what can be fully given over, is required.
