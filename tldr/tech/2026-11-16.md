[### Making a programming language (7 minute read)](https://alfonsocampodonico.com/posts/making-a-programming-language?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

A compiler is a line of small programs. Each turns one shape into the next. The programs transform text into tokens and tokens into a tree, then the tree gets checked, and either an interpreter runs it or it becomes a native binary. No single stage is hard - the work is in making errors useful and keeping two backends in agreement.

[### AI, work from home, and the young-graduate job market (15 minute read)](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/ai-work-from-home-and-the-young-graduate?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

New college graduates are employed less than a few years ago. While AI may be a factor, there's growing evidence that work from home is an important factor moving the needle on young-graduate employment. AI exposure and teleworkability are highly correlated with each other, making it hard to distinguish definitively between the two factors. The full extent of AI's effects on the labor market might not manifest until the next recession.

[### The avalanche that hit math is coming for AI development (50 minute read)](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

AI's capabilities in mathematics exploded quickly. Current AIs can now complete well-scoped problems in mathematics easily. This ability to solve well-scoped problems is going to be an even bigger deal for machine learning research than for mathematics. AI labs mainly care about how any new algorithmic recipe empirically makes training more efficient or elicits smarter models - they don't care about theory for its own sake.
