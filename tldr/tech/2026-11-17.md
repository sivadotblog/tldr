[### When will humans actually fly on Starship? (3 minute read)](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/when-will-humans-actually-fly-on-starship/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

Starship is now rapidly moving toward rapid reusability, but all estimates of human spaceflight on the vehicle should be taken with a gigantic grain of salt. There will be different timelines for humans flying aboard Starship and landing on Earth. The first people to fly on Starship probably won't launch from or land on Earth, as they will probably be NASA's lunar astronauts. Humans will likely not land on Earth in Starship before 2030.

[### Researchers track whole body movement with just a smartwatch (6 minute read)](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/11/researchers-track-whole-body-movement-just-smartwatch?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

EchoMotion is a technology that can track the movement of a person's entire body using ultrasonic sound waves with just an off-the-shelf smartwatch and a lightweight AI model. The system uses the built-in speaker and microphone on a commercial smartwatch to determine a person's body position using a sonar-like sensing system. The technology could potentially be used for tracking workouts, daily activities, and progress on physical rehabilitation. The EchoMotion team is currently developing the technology for use in fitness tracking and exploring the use of the technology in combination with first-person vision on smart glasses to efficiently track the objects that a person handles.

[### US moves to cut China's remote access to AI chips (9 minute read)](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/us-moves-to-cut-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

The US Commerce Department is closing a loophole that previously allowed foreign rivals to bypass export restrictions on advanced computer chips by renting access to them from data centers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil. Companies headquartered in countries deemed to present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion to China, Russia, and Iran must now apply for a license with strict know-your-customer controls when transferring high volumes of advanced chips. Companies with a license must submit monthly reports on transactions that thoroughly detail all parties involved. The rule change will take effect 90 days after it's posted to the Federal Register.
