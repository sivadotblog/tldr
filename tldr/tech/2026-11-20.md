### Science & Futuristic Technology

[### Google turns to Blue Origin for a rocket, but its long-term problem remains (4 minute read)](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/google-turns-to-blue-origin-for-a-rocket-but-its-long-term-problem-remains/?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket will launch data center satellites for Google in 2029. Google's Project Suncatcher program is a long-term moonshot that aims to scale AI infrastructure in space by tapping into solar power. Its satellites each have the computing capacity of roughly the scale of a data center rack. The mission on New Glenn will deploy satellites into a Sun-synchronous orbit, where they will be able to receive nearly continuous solar energy.

[### Spacecraft reaches Mercury on second-ever mission to enter its orbit (4 minute read)](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/20/spacecraft-mercury-second-ever-mission-orbit-bepicolombo?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

The BepiColombo mission is now preparing to swing into orbit around Mercury. The joint European-Japanese space mission aims to help scientists understand how Mercury formed so close to the Sun, why it has a magnetic field, and what makes up the frozen ice under the planet's permanently shadowed craters. The spacecraft will separate into two orbiters in early December. The orbiters will maneuver to different polar orbits around the planet, and science operations will begin in April next year.

[### The most important number in AI (42 minute read)](https://asteriskmag.substack.com/p/the-most-important-number-in-ai?utm_source=tldrnewsletter)

The Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) is a measure of AI progress developed by Epoch AI, a nonprofit research organization. It provides a single number that summarizes how well AI models do on benchmarks. It is designed to be continuously updatable, and researchers can use it to look at trends going back years and forecast things for future models. This post covers how the ECI is constructed, the challenges of benchmark selection, whether it makes sense to talk about 'model IQ', whether static benchmarks stay relevant as models get better, and much more.
