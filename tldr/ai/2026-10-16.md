### Headlines & Launches

[### Muse Spark Open (4 minute read)](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open?utm_source=tldrai)

Muse Spark Open is an 816B-parameter multimodal open-weight model with 42B active parameters and a 1M-token context window. It was designed for coding and long-horizon agentic tasks and can run locally across two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios using MLX.

[### Inside Microsoft's Frontier Pre-Training Stack (18 minute read)](https://microsoft.ai/news/frontier-pre-training-at-microsoft-ai/?utm_source=tldrai)

Microsoft detailed the training of MAI-Base-2, a 2.8T-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 80B active parameters that it said surpassed every open-weight model in its held-out pre-training evaluations. The deep dive covers its data strategy, compute efficiency, full-stack infrastructure, and Microsoft's plans to turn internally trained frontier models into enterprise offerings through Foundry.

[### d1-ultra: Introducing the World's Best Decision Model (3 minute read)](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-ultra?utm_source=tldrai)

Liquid AI's d1-ultra is a multimodal decision model designed for complex real-world tasks like detecting defects on circuit boards. The model processes text and images and provides better-calibrated answers and higher accuracy than previous models like Jev 1.14. Available on the Liquid API, d1-ultra is priced at $0.042 per million input tokens and will soon be accessible via Vercel and OpenRouter.

### Deep Dives & Analysis

[### Neural Networks Are Already Clean (65 minute read)](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/?utm_source=tldrai)

The cause of polysemanticity in early model organisms was thought to be superposition. This convinced much of the field that networks pack concepts arbitrarily, but what looked like arbitrary packing was mostly recursive generalization, which is less concerning. This means networks can be studied directly, instead of needing to be untangled first. The outlook for mechanistic interpretability may be far better than we thought.

[### The Gradient Is a Covariance (8 minute read)](https://richardli.xyz/post/gradient-is-covariance/?utm_source=tldrai)

Reinforcement learning, on-policy distillation, and supervised fine-tuning on near-policy data change only a small fraction of parameters. Small and sparse are different claims. The expected gradient is a covariance, the Fisher is a variance, and the Fisher caps the gradient for any reward. At one position, the Fisher is the variance of logit sensitivities: whatever moves every candidate together counts for nothing. None of this needs on-policy sampling - it needs a score centered under the sampling distribution.

[### Mo' turns, mo' problems (6 minute read)](https://ianbarber.blog/2026/10/15/mo-turns-mo-problems/?utm_source=tldrai)

Doing reinforcement learning for agent use-cases is conceptually not at all that complicated. For each problem, you set up a VM with a harness and a fresh model context, send the prompt, let the model make tool calls, score the whole thing at the end, and update the weights. Agentic post-training means there's more than the model to consider. Even when the harness is consistent, you can still go off-policy with the trajectories themselves. Agents spawn subagents, make parallel tool calls, and compact large sessions, which adds complexity.
