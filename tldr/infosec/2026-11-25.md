### Attacks & Vulnerabilities

[### 1.5 Million Impacted by Data Breach at South Korean Electric Company KEPCO (1 minute read)](https://www.securityweek.com/1-5-million-impacted-by-data-breach-at-south-korean-electric-company-kepco/?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

KEPCO says an external attacker exfiltrated data from its KEPCO ON customer portal and supplier relationship management platform. Records for 650,487 customers include names, contact details, ID numbers, bank accounts, card details, and billing information. Another 869,761 SRM records expose supplier and employee contact and employment data. KEPCO suspended affected services and blocked external access.

[### Hackers Exploit Critical ASP.NET Anti-CSRF Library Flaw Allowing Unauthenticated RCE (3 minute read)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-critical-aspnet-anti.html?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

Attackers are exploiting a critical insecure deserialization flaw in Idunno.AntiCsrf, an ASP.NET WebForms library abandoned since 2008, where one unauthenticated POST carrying a Base64-encoded ObjectStateFormatter gadget chain achieves RCE as w3wp.exe (CVE-2026-131914, CVSS 9.3). Mandiant found the bug and confirmed in-the-wild exploitation, but no patch is coming for versions 0.9.1.0 and 0.9.1 Beta. Defenders should search Web.config files and bin/ directories for AntiCsrfModule references, replace the library with System.Web.Helpers.AntiForgery or ASP.NET Core IAntiforgery, and rotate machineKey and other secrets if IIS logs show unusual .aspx POSTs returning 302 or 500.

[### Turnitin discloses cyberattack affecting customer-facing systems (2 minute read)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/turnitin-discloses-cyberattack-affecting-customer-facing-systems/?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

Turnitin, an education software provider used by more than 16,000 institutions and 71 million students, detected unauthorized activity on some customer-facing systems and has engaged outside responders, law enforcement, and regulators. The company hasn't disclosed how attackers gained access, when the activity occurred, or whether student or institutional data was taken. It has said only that the affected systems contain no government IDs or financial data, and no ransomware group has claimed responsibility. Until Turnitin discloses the scope, institutions should watch for phishing attempts using Turnitin as a lure and review their SSO and LMS integration logs for unusual access.

### Strategies & Tactics

[### Brake Check: Hijacking Trailer Brakes Over Bluetooth (39 minute read)](https://elementdigital.com.au/blog/brake-check-trailer-brakes-over-bluetooth/?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

Researchers found that Elecbrakes EB2, VM1, and ELBC 2000 trailer brake controllers accepted unauthenticated Bluetooth commands. A nearby phone or laptop could write four bytes to activate, modulate, or release trailer brakes without pairing, a PIN, or authorization. The force byte controlled braking strength, and nearly every non-zero opcode activated the brakes. Commands lacked replay protection, and a persistent register retained the last command. The firmware-update channel used Just Works pairing, CRC32 integrity checks, and an app-distributed decryption key. Elecbrakes fixed EB2 and VM1 on July 6. ELBC 2000 remains unpatched. CISA assigned CVE-2026-96805, CVE-2026-125273, and CVE-2026-124281.

[### AI Agents Are Creating a Shared Responsibility Gap (3 minute read)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ai-agents-are-creating-a-shared-responsibility-gap/?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

Shared responsibility models have been a staple framework for SaaS and cloud services, delineating which aspects a provider is responsible for and which a user is responsible for. Harnesses and the MCP spec both put the responsibility and audit logging for sensitive actions on the user. Agent identities also do not provide a silver bullet. For example, Microsoft's documentation for Entra agent identities also puts responsibility for sensitive actions back on the user. Organizations must accept that responsibility cannot be delegated and that agents require clear owners, least privilege, and consistent logging.

[### Judge the Cyber Apocalypse by the Data, Not the Headlines (15 minute read)](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/judge-the-cyber-apocalypse-by-the-data-not-the-headlines?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

CSET's Andrew Lohn finds that while the technical indicators favor attackers, with monthly CVEs tripling since Claude Mythos launched, slower patching, and exploitation now behind 31% of breaches, data on real-world impact looks steady or better, since significant incidents, healthcare breach victims, breach costs, and ransomware payouts are all flat or down. He explains that fear of attribution and retaliation, not technical capability, may have always limited nation-states and criminals. He recommends tougher enforcement, investment in attribution and resilience, and hardening critical systems for a crisis where those limits fall away, but he concedes that some datasets lag and the trend could still turn.

[### OpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo (4 minute read)](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo/?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

Shirudo is an open-source eBPF runtime from OpenAI and Cisco that deploys temporary "shields" to block a vulnerable code path on a running Linux system without a restart or a patch. In a test, a shield generated by Codex added an input check to Java's Base64 decoder and stopped a Java4Shell exploit against a live Elasticsearch process. Shields are cryptographically signed, checked by the eBPF verifier, and recorded through TPM attestation, which makes Shirudo a promising stopgap for teams with long patch cycles, though the case for AI-generated shields so far rests on a single controlled demo.

### Miscellaneous

[### Here's Why Data Breach Disclosure is so Hard: Lessons from Hundreds of Emails with Hacked Companies (13 minute read)](https://www.troyhunt.com/heres-why-data-breach-disclosure-is-so-hard-lessons-from-hundreds-of-emails-with-hacked-companies/?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

Troy Hunt reviewed 8.5 years of breach-notification emails sent to 73 organizations. Common responses included no security contact, denial before reviewing evidence, open-ended investigations, legal control of wording, and arguments that disclosure itself causes harm. In one case, leaked records covered 16 user-system tables, including identity-verification and tax documents. Effective organizations replied quickly, investigated the evidence, notified customers, and published updates.

[### What 5,400 Malicious npm Packages Reveal about Software Supply Chain Risk in 2026 (11 minute read)](https://www.databricks.com/blog/what-5400-malicious-npm-packages-reveal-about-software-supply-chain-risk-2026?utm_source=tldrinfosec)

A review of about 5,400 malicious npm packages from the past year found that compromised legitimate packages made up only 7% of the set but more than 99% of downloads, while the attacker-created packages that make up most of the malware were rarely installed at all. After npm revoked long-lived tokens, attackers moved to compromising CI pipelines and publishing through OIDC trusted publishing, and 38% of packages hid their C2 traffic in legitimate services such as Cloudflare Workers and Discord webhooks. The registry took a median of 7 days to remove malicious packages, while attackers often deleted their own within hours.
